What the Cat

Privacy Policy

For the Android app published on Google Play · Effective 2 October 2026

What the Cat does not collect anything. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, and no server belonging to this app. Everything you log stays in the app's private storage on your phone.

What the app stores

Only what you type or choose yourself:

  • your cats' names and, if you add them, their photos;
  • the incidents you log — date, time, type and any observations you write;
  • the incident types you create and the order you put them in.

All of it is written to the app's own private storage, which only this app can read. None of it is transmitted anywhere.

What the app does not do

  • It does not ask you to create an account or sign in.
  • It does not contain analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking of any kind.
  • It does not send your data to the developer or to anyone else — this version makes no network requests at all.
  • It asks for no runtime permissions. Photos come from the system picker, which hands the app only the image you pick.

Device backup

One thing happens outside the app's control. If you have Android's backup turned on, the operating system may include this app's data in the backup of your device to your own Google account, the same way it does for other apps. That is a feature of Android, handled by Google under their privacy policy, not something this app sends or can read. You can turn it off for all apps in your phone's backup settings.

Getting your data out, and deleting it

The History tab exports your full log as a CSV file, which you can keep or send to a vet. Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored — export first if you want to keep it. There is no copy anywhere else to delete, and no one to ask.

Children

The app is not directed to children and collects nothing from anyone, regardless of age.

Changes

If a future version of the app adds optional sign-in and cloud sync, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described here with a new effective date.
